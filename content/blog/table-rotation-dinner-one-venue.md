# Table rotation dinner: mix your guests every course in one venue

**A table rotation dinner keeps everyone in the same room, but shuffles the seating every course: new table, new faces.** It takes the best part of a running dinner – meeting new people over food – and fits it into a single venue: a clubhouse, function room or village hall. No addresses, no walking routes, just numbered tables and a clever seating plan.

## Why rotate tables at all?

At a classic sit-down dinner, you speak to the two people next to you and nobody else. At a club dinner or gala that is a wasted opportunity: the whole point of the evening is that members mix. With a rotation over three courses, each guest sits with two entirely new groups – at a 60-person dinner you genuinely talk to a dozen people instead of two.

It works brilliantly for:

- **Club and society dinners** – new and old members actually meet
- **Galas and anniversary dinners** – tables stop being islands
- **Networking events** – structured mingling without the awkwardness
- **New Year's receptions and team dinners** – everyone speaks to everyone

## How it works in practice

1. **Number your tables.** The table size follows from your room and group: 6 to 8 per table works best.
2. **Make a seating plan per course.** Starter, main and dessert each get their own layout, designed so that nobody shares a table with the same person twice.
3. **Show guests their route.** Each guest gets a personal card: "Starter – table 3, Main – table 7, Dessert – table 2".
4. **Rotate between courses.** While plates are cleared, everyone finds their next table. Two minutes of pleasant chaos, then fresh conversations.

## Tips from practice

- **Announce the rotation in the invitation** so nobody "claims" a seat for the night.
- **Keep couples together if they prefer** – a good plan can pin pairs while still rotating everyone else.
- **Put the course schedule on the wall** as well as the personal cards; it saves questions.
- **End with a shared final course or quiz** at fixed tables – by then everyone has met, and the room feels like one group.
