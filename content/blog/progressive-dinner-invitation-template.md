# Progressive dinner invitation: template, wording and checklist

The invitation decides whether 6 or 16 couples join. It has to explain in thirty seconds what a progressive dinner is, why it is fun — and what you need to do to take part. Here is a template that has proven itself, plus the points no invitation should miss.

## What must be in the invitation

- **Date, start time and rough end** (e.g. "Saturday 18 October, 6:30 pm until late")
- **The principle in two sentences** — many people have never heard of it
- **Who can join** (couples, two-person teams, singles with a travel companion?)
- **What it costs** — usually nothing but the groceries for your own course
- **Sign-up deadline** and how to sign up
- **Which details you need**: address, mobile number, allergies/dietary needs, preferred course, maximum number of guests at home
- **Where the closing party is**

## The template (WhatsApp or email)

<blockquote class="invite-template" style="margin:20px 0;padding:18px 22px;border-left:4px solid #E85D3A;background:#FFF9F5;border-radius:8px">
<p><strong>🍽️ Progressive dinner in [place] – Saturday [date]</strong></p>
<p>Hi all! We're organising a progressive dinner: every couple cooks one course at home and is a guest for the other two courses with neighbours they may not know yet. Three courses, three addresses, lots of new faces — and at the end we all celebrate together.</p>
<p><strong>Schedule:</strong> 6:30 pm starter · 7:45 pm main · 9:15 pm dessert · from 10:30 pm closing party at [venue]</p>
<p><strong>Cost:</strong> none — everyone only pays for the groceries of their own course.</p>
<p><strong>Joining?</strong> Sign up before [deadline] via [link/reply] with:<br>– your names and address<br>– mobile number (for the route on the night)<br>– allergies or dietary requirements<br>– preferred course (starter / main / dessert) — optional<br>– how many guests fit at your table at most</p>
<p>You only find out on the night where you're going — that's the fun of it. 😉</p>
<p>Questions? Just get in touch! [name + number]</p>
</blockquote>

Feel free to shorten it. The three things that must never be missing: date, sign-up deadline, allergy question.

## Wording for special occasions

**Neighbourhood / street:** "We've lived on the same street for years and hardly know each other — let's change that in one evening."

**Club or society:** "Once right across all departments: at the progressive dinner the youth coach sits next to the treasurer."

**Birthday or anniversary:** "Instead of one party in one place: you travel to me — or I travel to you, course by course."

**Student society / shared houses:** "Three courses, three houses, one evening. Who makes the best dessert?"

## The checklist up to the deadline

1. **6 weeks ahead**: set the date, book the closing venue, send the invitation.
2. **4 weeks ahead**: remind everyone who hasn't replied. Now estimate whether the number of teams will be divisible by three.
3. **3 weeks ahead — sign-up deadline**: check the list: addresses complete? Allergies noted? Course preferences spread out?
4. **2 weeks ahead**: assign courses and plan the seating — who sits with whom, without repeat encounters, with short routes. This is the part a planner does in seconds instead of evenings.
5. **1 week ahead**: inform hosts (how many guests, which dietary needs). Print envelope cards or prepare digital routes.
6. **On the day**: hand out routes, phone on, prepare the closing party.

## Common invitation mistakes

- **No deadline** — then replies trickle in until the day before and the planning is never finished.
- **Allergies not asked** — hosts find out at the front door.
- **Too long** — nobody reads six paragraphs on WhatsApp. Principle, date, sign-up, done.
- **No mobile numbers** — worth gold on the night when someone can't find the door.

## Conclusion

A good progressive dinner invitation is short, answers the three questions "what, when, how do I sign up?" and asks about allergies right away. After that the real work starts: turning the sign-up list into a seating plan that gives everyone new faces and short routes.
