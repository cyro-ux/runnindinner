# Progressive dinner menu ideas: recipes per course that can wait

At a progressive dinner you cook for guests you don't know, who arrive at a time that can easily shift by 20 minutes — and who have to move on straight after. That makes different demands than a normal dinner. The dishes that work here share three traits: they can be **prepared ahead**, they can **wait**, and they go **on the table in one go**.

## The ground rule: prepare, don't cook

As a host you want to sit at the table, not stand at the stove. Ideal is a dish that is 90 percent done when the doorbell rings: kept warm in the oven, served cold or plated in five minutes. Anything that has to be cooked to order — steak, risotto, soufflé — does not belong on a progressive dinner table.

## Starter (60 minutes, usually 6 people)

The starter is the most relaxed course: guests arrive on time because it is the first stop.

- **Gazpacho or chilled cucumber-yoghurt soup** (summer) — made the day before, served from the fridge. Vegan and gluten-free without effort.
- **Pumpkin soup with ginger** (autumn/winter) — sits on a low flame and gets better by the minute.
- **Bruschetta board with three toppings** — tomato-basil, ricotta-honey, white beans with rosemary. Toast the bread when the bell rings; toppings are ready.
- **Cured salmon or beetroot carpaccio** — fully prepared, just plate.

**Budget tip:** soup costs under two euros per person and still feels like a proper course.

## Main course (75 minutes, the demanding one)

Here guests arrive late and have to leave on time. The main must be able to wait **and** be on the table fast.

- **Oven dishes**: lasagne (classic or spinach-ricotta), moussaka, chicken thighs with roasted vegetables. At 80 °C everything stays perfect for an hour.
- **Braises and stews**: beef goulash, coq au vin, lentil dal or a chickpea curry. They taste better on day two — so cook the day before.
- **Tagine or ratatouille with couscous** — vegetarian, gluten-free (with rice instead of couscous) and fully preparable.
- **Big salad bowl with a warm component** (summer) — baked sweet potato, halloumi or grilled chicken strips made in advance.

**Rule of thumb for dietary needs:** cook the main vegetarian and offer meat or fish as a separate side. One dish then covers almost every reported requirement without cooking two menus.

## Dessert (60 minutes, the sociable round)

Dessert is often the longest stop, because only the closing party follows. Sweet but uncomplicated is the way.

- **Tiramisu or panna cotta** — portioned in glasses, made hours ahead.
- **Fruit crumble with vanilla ice cream** — the crumble goes into the oven 20 minutes before the bell; the smell welcomes the guests.
- **Cheese board with grapes, figs and nuts** — zero cooking, pairs with a glass of wine that stretches the round.
- **Chocolate mousse** (vegan with aquafaba works) — prepared the day before.

Have coffee and tea ready: your guests have two courses and two journeys behind them.

## Allergies and diets without stress

You learn in advance how many guests with which needs are coming — the planning should list that per table. Three pragmatic rules:

1. **Gluten-free** is easier at the main than you'd think: rice, potatoes, polenta and pulses instead of pasta and bread.
2. **Vegan** is simplest at the main with curry, dal or tagine — and at dessert with sorbet or fruit.
3. **Nut allergy** — check pesto, crumble toppings and desserts; serve nuts separately in a bowl.

## Keeping an eye on the budget

A progressive dinner costs every host only their own course. Realistically: starter 2–4 euros, main 5–8 euros, dessert 2–3 euros per person. Choose soup, a stew or a crumble and you stay under 40 euros for a table of six — and still enjoy a three-course evening.

## Conclusion

The best progressive dinner recipe is the one that lets you sit at the table: prepared, wait-proof, served in one go. With soup, an oven dish and a dessert in a glass almost nothing can go wrong — and your guests will remember the conversations, not the foam on the plate.
