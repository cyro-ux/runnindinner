# Safari supper: what it is and how to organise one

**A safari supper is a dinner party where guests "migrate" between houses for each course, meeting different people at every table.** Each couple cooks one course at home and is a guest for the others. In the Netherlands and Germany the same format is called a running dinner; in North America it goes by progressive dinner. Different names, same wonderful evening.

## How a safari supper works

- Each couple (or pair) cooks **one course** at home and joins the **other courses** as guests elsewhere.
- Between courses everyone walks (or drives, in rural areas) to the next address – the "safari".
- The seating is shuffled every course, so you meet new faces at every table.
- Where you are heading next is traditionally kept secret until the end of each course – classic with sealed envelopes, nowadays also digital.
- The evening ends with everyone together for dessert or drinks at one final location.

A safari supper works from about 9 couples (18 people) and gets really lively with 12 to 24 couples. With three courses, the number of hosting couples should be divisible by three.

## The three classic rules

1. **Nobody meets the same table companions twice.** The whole point is mixing – the seating plan should guarantee it.
2. **Hosts know how many are coming and what dietary needs they have – not who.** The surprise is half the fun.
3. **Keep the distances short.** More time at the table than on the road: aim for under 10 minutes between addresses.

## Organising one: the step-by-step plan

1. **Pick a date and send the invitation.** Ask for address, phone number, allergies and course preference right away.
2. **Close sign-ups three weeks ahead** and check the numbers work for your course count.
3. **Make the seating and route plan.** This is the hard part: who cooks which course, who eats where, nobody meeting twice, distances staying walkable. By hand this is an evening of puzzling per attempt – [a planner does it in one click](/en/blog/how-to-organise-a-progressive-dinner).
4. **Brief the hosts** on guest counts and dietary requirements.
5. **Hand out the routes** – printed envelope cards opened per course, or digital reveals.
6. **Enjoy the evening.** As the organiser you simply take part.

## Safari supper in a single venue

No walkable village, or a club that wants everyone under one roof? You can run the same format inside one hall: everyone stays in the room and moves to a different numbered table each course, always with new table companions. A sailing club recently ran their annual dinner this way for 60 members – three courses, table rotation, and a quiz to finish.
